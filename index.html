01 · Project Overview

The Clinical Challenge

Hospital readmission within 30 days is a critical quality indicator in healthcare — and a costly problem. This project addresses the challenge of predicting diabetes patient readmission from messy, real-world electronic health records (EHR).

Using the UCI Diabetes 130-US Hospitals dataset spanning 10 years of clinical care (1999–2008), our team built an end-to-end ML pipeline capable of handling severe class imbalance, high missingness, and noisy categorical data.

Three models were compared: Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, and Random Forest. Each model was evaluated on AUC-ROC, F1-Score, Precision, Recall, and Accuracy — with particular attention to clinical utility over raw metrics.

03 · Exploratory Analysis

Interactive Visualizations

📊 PATIENT AGE DISTRIBUTION — The dataset skews heavily toward older patients, with the [70-80] age group forming the largest cohort (~26,000 patients). This reflects diabetes prevalence in elderly populations and informs model bias considerations.
🔴 MISSING VALUE RATE PER COLUMN — Weight (97% missing) and medical_specialty (49%) were dropped or imputed. A 40% threshold line guided feature retention decisions, ensuring data quality before modeling.
📈 NUMERIC FEATURE DISTRIBUTIONS — time_in_hospital shows a right-skewed distribution peaking at 1-3 days. num_lab_procedures follows a near-normal distribution centered at ~45. num_medications peaks around 14-16. number_inpatient is heavily zero-inflated.
🌡 CORRELATION HEATMAP — Strongest positive correlations: num_medications ↔ time_in_hospital (0.47), num_procedures ↔ num_medications (0.39). Emergency and inpatient visits correlate moderately (0.27). Most features show weak inter-correlation, suggesting diverse predictive signal.
🤖 MODEL COMPARISON — Logistic Regression and Random Forest tie on most metrics (Accuracy ~66%, F1 ~71%, Precision ~79%). Decision Tree underperforms significantly. LR and RF both show AUC-ROC ~0.59, indicating class imbalance remains a challenge.
⚡ SCALABILITY ANALYSIS — Random Forest training time scales near-linearly from 7.74s (25% = 22,903 rows) to 9.76s (100% = 91,560 rows). Excellent scalability for a full ensemble model, showing sub-linear growth relative to data volume.
04 · Key Findings

What We Discovered

🧬
FINDING 01
Elderly Dominance
70-80 year olds form the largest patient cohort, driving strong age-based feature importance.
The dataset is heavily concentrated in the 60-80 age range. This demographic reality means models must be robust to age-correlated comorbidities, polypharmacy, and extended hospital stays. Age group encoding proved a valuable feature in all three models.
+ READ MORE
💊
FINDING 02
Medications Matter
num_medications shows the strongest correlation with time_in_hospital (r=0.47), a key predictor.
Patients on more medications tend to stay longer — reflecting disease complexity. This feature also correlated with num_procedures (r=0.39), suggesting a cluster of "high-intervention" patients who may need enhanced post-discharge follow-up to prevent readmission.
+ READ MORE
🚨
FINDING 03
Data Quality Crisis
Weight feature was 97% missing — unusable. Three columns exceeded the 40% drop threshold.
Weight, medical_specialty, and payer_code all had missingness above 40% and were dropped. This highlights the systemic data quality challenges in real-world EHR systems. Imputation strategies were applied for race (~2% missing) using mode substitution.
+ READ MORE
🌲
FINDING 04
Forest Wins Robustness
Random Forest tied Logistic Regression on metrics but showed significantly better scalability and noise resilience.
Despite near-identical metric scores, Random Forest demonstrated consistent performance across different data fractions and proved more resistant to outliers. Its ensemble nature makes it the recommended choice for production clinical environments where data quality is unpredictable.
+ READ MORE
⚖️
FINDING 05
Class Imbalance Challenge
The readmission (<30 days) class was severely underrepresented — requiring SMOTE oversampling.
The target variable had significant imbalance, with early readmissions forming a minority class. SMOTE was applied to generate synthetic samples for the minority class. Despite this, AUC-ROC scores (~0.59) indicate the problem remains challenging — a finding common in clinical readmission prediction literature.
+ READ MORE
⚡
FINDING 06
Linear Scalability
Training time grew from 7.74s to 9.76s as data quadrupled — demonstrating excellent computational efficiency.
Going from 22,903 to 91,560 rows only added ~2 seconds of training time. This sub-linear scaling suggests the Random Forest implementation is well-optimized and could handle much larger datasets without prohibitive compute costs — making it suitable for hospital-scale deployments.
